## Coordinate and redevelopment choices

The main runway is drawn along local +X. Sunshine Coast uses its published 308° magnetic orientation in this direction; Gold Coast uses 139°. Other sites use the rounded runway designator direction. The north vector includes reference east magnetic variation: 8° at Hamilton Island and 11° at the southeast Queensland sites. The fictional inland site uses 11° as a visual convention. These are orientation cues, not navigation precision.

Runway length is compressed to 300 world units at the game's maximum developed length, except Queensland Gateway at 540 units, Brisbane and Hamilton Island at 420 units and Gold Coast at 498.4 units. Reference secondary runway lengths keep approximately the real ratio to their primary runway. Runway widths and aircraft dimensions share `metresToWorld`: 300/853 at Redcliffe; 0.5 at Archerfield; 0.12 at Queensland Gateway; 1/3 at Hamilton Island; 0.2 at Sunshine Coast and Gold Coast; 420/3560 at Brisbane.

Hamilton Island was revised on 8 September 2026: runway 14 runs along +X (140° magnetic), with the northeast-side terminal near its northern end. The E/A/B/C links and opposite-side D spur follow the chart's relative topology, with a larger apron for seven game plots. Arrivals use B or a shorter-runway interim exit instead of a full-length parallel loop. See [the Hamilton Island plan](HAMILTON-ISLAND-LAYOUT-PLAN.md) for scope, source dates and intentional game adaptations.

The main centreline is Y=116. At the six other sites, arrival/departure lanes are Y=58/82, stands face the terminal at Y=28 with pitches of at least 24 world units, and the hold-short line is [6,96] with aircraft centres stopping at [6,86]. Brisbane uses B at Y=79 for arrivals, A at Y=93 for departures, a [6,108] hold-short line and [6,102] stopping point. Its twelve individually positioned stands have pitches of 20–22 world units and smaller interactive footprints. `layouts.mjs` stand paths describe the fully extended baseline; `traffic.mjs` generates curved routes for the current developed runway length. Class-based animated taxiway widths are 7/15/18/23/25/30 reference metres multiplied by the site's scale. These distances provide game visual separation, not regulatory clearances.

Brisbane uses 420 / 3560 world units per metre for geography, aircraft and pavement widths. Its western runway starts 178 units north of the eastern threshold (approximately 1.51 km along the calibrated chart axis), sits 236 units west (about 2 km), and is exactly 3300 / 3560 of the eastern reference length. Twelve stable plots are distributed 4 international / 6 domestic / 2 general aviation; these are fictional game allocations, not real gate counts or stand numbers.

`brisbane-reference.mjs` records source points independently of `brisbane-layout.mjs`. The aerodrome PDF's eastern runway polygon end midpoints [214.15,467.826] and [329,243.376], measured in native page points from the top left, calibrate translation, rotation and uniform scale to [0,116] and [420,116]. Twelve building/precinct centres (including the three domestic lounges) and two apron points are checked against that transform. One PDF point corresponds to roughly 14 metres: small computed centre errors are drawing agreement, not metre-level survey accuracy. Per-anchor tolerances of 3–10 world units allow schematic building extents and manual interpretation.

The international terminal sits south of the eastern 01R threshold. Domestic headhouse, crescent, connecting concourses and three circular lounges follow the chart and operator illustration; two parking decks and the domestic Airtrain station sit on their landside. General aviation beside the northern end of 01R/19L is separate from the farther-north remote apron. Three airline maintenance buildings and the general aviation maintenance precinct occupy the eastern side; the tower has its own measured anchor. Roof details, individual building edges, car parks, road loops, rail supports and shorelines are hand-authored approximations.

The renderer consumes the 85 named authored taxiway paths: A/B/C and their apron/runway connectors; western S/T; cross-field Y/Z/W; northern D/E/F/G; and eastern H/M. The remote apron reaches D/E instead of an invented S shortcut. Designators explicitly marked unused by the source chart are omitted. Curves and some branch positions are simplified; reference widths use 30 metres uniformly, rather than representing every published width or code restriction. Northern ground access passes beneath Y/Z. Eastern runway connectors appear only when the developed runway reaches them. Local stand lead-ins and changing development-stage exits support the game animation; they are not a reproduction of operational aircraft routing.

Research and cargo upgrades are fictional buildings in illustrative landside/logistics areas. The handling upgrade uses the real general aviation building precinct as an anchor, but its gameplay function is fictional. Only the developed eastern runway carries animated traffic. The 4000 m Brisbane upgrade ceiling remains a fictional game length, distinct from the 3560 m real-world reference; no operational clearances or code restrictions are simulated.

Only the main runway currently carries animated aircraft. Archerfield's grass crosses are marked closed **within the fictional redevelopment scenario**; the real airport continues to list them. Its second sealed runway, Gold Coast's short cross strip and Brisbane's western parallel remain visual references. No claim is made that these real runways are closed. The island airport shoreline has been widened around game taxiways to keep the redevelopment on land.

Game surface levels, length limits, fleet permissions and towers are independent of real airport certification.
